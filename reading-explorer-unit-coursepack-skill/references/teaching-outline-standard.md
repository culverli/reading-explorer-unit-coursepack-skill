# Concise Teaching Outline Standard

## Role

Create a usable route for the teacher, not a full scripted Teacher Guide and not merely an exercise list.

Respect the teacher's intended flow. The structure below defines the information to include, not a fixed classroom sequence.

## Opening page

Include:

- article title, Reading Explorer level, reading mode, lesson count, and lesson minutes;
- a compact Unit route table;
- the main vocabulary, phrases, sentence frames, and final product;
- a clear classwork/homework allocation.

Unless the teacher says otherwise, route a small number of pivotal exercises to class and the majority of Practice Book exercise groups to homework. Classroom time should model the thinking or language move students need before independent work.

## Lesson record

For each lesson include:

- lesson number and a meaningful title;
- one-sentence focus;
- timed stages that add up to the stated lesson length;
- concise teacher action and observable student result;
- Practice Book classwork;
- homework with an estimated completion time;
- how the homework will be checked or reused next lesson;
- a compact board plan;
- one useful ready-to-say English line when wording matters.

Five stages often fit a 40-minute lesson, but do not force five when the teacher's flow or the article needs another structure.

## Flexible design rules

- Do not make every lesson follow Lead-in → Teach → Practise → Exit.
- Allow a grammar cloze to open an extensive-reading lesson when the teacher chooses that route.
- Allow source questions to be spread across lessons rather than completed in printed order.
- Use classwork to demonstrate, compare, explain, rehearse, or correct; use homework for most independent completion and delayed practice.
- Let lesson count determine exercise volume. Do not squeeze a full intensive-reading book into a two-lesson extensive-reading sequence.
- Include only activities that serve the reading or language purpose. Games and group work are optional.

## Homework balance

Default to `majority_homework` at the exercise-group level, not by counting individual blanks. A helpful route is:

- class: pivotal multiple choice, one deeper short-answer set, one vocabulary-in-sentence set, one sentence-repair/transform set, one summary/rebuild task, or final-output planning;
- home: the remaining comprehension, word/phrase search, form, combination, translation, review, and final writing tasks after they have been prepared.

Do not assign a task type students have not seen or prepared. Do not assign the exact same completed classroom item again.

## Calibration

For intensive reading, allocate more time to rereading, Bloom questions, language mining, sentence practice, and cumulative output.

For extensive reading, protect fast reading and gist. Keep pre-reading cloze brief, comprehension light, vocabulary selective, and follow-up proportionate.

## Acceptance test

The outline is ready when a teacher can see, within a few minutes:

- why each lesson exists;
- what happens in class;
- which exercises are homework;
- what students produce;
- how the next lesson begins from previous work.
