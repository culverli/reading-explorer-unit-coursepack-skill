# Teacher Guide Standard

## Purpose

Create a concise but complete pre-class operating guide. A teacher should understand the next lesson in 10–20 minutes without inventing the lead-in, questions, examples, activity rules, answers, board plan, or homework check.

The guide is not an exercise-routing sample and not a curriculum report. It must contain every lesson's full 40-minute design.

## Unit-Level Opening

Include:

- learner and class constraints;
- Unit language and reading role;
- source/material boundary;
- Unit lesson route and approximate period allocation;
- Practice Book section/exercise map;
- electronic textbook, board, print, audio/video, and optional activity-sheet requirements;
- active-language and grammar priorities;
- homework and assessment route.

## Required Lesson Record

For every lesson, include:

1. `Lesson N · Learner-facing title`.
2. Purpose and observable success evidence.
3. Human-readable source and Practice Book route.
4. Materials and concrete preparation.
5. Complete timed stages totaling 38–42 minutes.
6. A specific introduction/lead-in.
7. Teacher moves in concise Chinese.
8. Exact ready-to-say English for questions, directions, models, frames, and board prompts where wording matters.
9. Student grouping, action, product, and individual evidence.
10. Checking move, likely error, and recovery move.
11. Transition to the next stage.
12. Board plan and electronic textbook/display route.
13. Optional activity/game instructions and low-preparation fallback when used.
14. Lesson close or exit evidence.
15. Homework assignment by Practice Book exercise number, estimated time, and oral verification if applicable.
16. Next-lesson homework check/use.
17. Answers, acceptable responses, or judgment criteria for every assessable task.

Do not replace these with labels such as `controlled practice`, `discussion`, or `check answers`. State the actual prompt, item route, product, and check.

## Timed Stage Contract

Each stage records:

- `stage_type` and learner-facing stage title;
- minutes;
- grouping;
- teacher action in Chinese;
- ready-to-say English where needed;
- student action and product;
- individual evidence;
- check and anticipated response;
- likely error and recovery;
- transition;
- board/display action;
- source and Practice Book references.

The first lesson stage must be a real introduction/lead-in. It must activate meaning, prediction, retrieval, or an expression need. Do not use attendance, lesson-goal reading, or decorative movement as the lead-in.

## Lesson Variation

Let purpose shape the lesson:

- meaning-first reading lessons emphasize first-pass purpose, gist, detail, evidence, inference, and text structure;
- close-study lessons recover sentence meaning, teach vocabulary/chunks at first encounter, notice useful form, practice accuracy, and build new use;
- consolidation lessons use closed-book retrieval, precision, repair, text building, retell, and transfer;
- Reading B lessons increase independence and selective language mining;
- video/listening lessons keep gist before detail and transcript support after listening;
- output lessons include model/noticing, planning, rehearsal or drafting, feedback, and revision.

Do not render every lesson with the same sequence of boxes or the same activity fingerprint.

## Ready-to-Say Language

Use Chinese for teacher operation and pedagogical judgment. Use English for language that will actually be spoken, displayed, modeled, or written on the board.

Avoid vague notes such as `Ask a question about the text`. Supply the question:

> Which sentence gives the shortest proof? Underline only the words you need.

Keep ready-to-say language compact. Do not script routine praise or every conversational filler.

## Board and Display

No fixed Classroom Display PPT is assumed.

For each lesson, identify:

- electronic textbook page, reading-body image, infographic, or video to display;
- words, chunks, sentence frames, evidence structure, retell route, and corrections to build on the board;
- what remains visible during practice;
- what students must record in the Practice Book before the display disappears.

## Activities and Games

Games are optional. When used, record:

- language purpose and prerequisite;
- setup/materials;
- grouping and roles;
- rules and time;
- scoring or right/wrong standard;
- evidence or repair demand;
- group product and individual evidence;
- teacher adjudication;
- fallback.

Reject movement with no additional thinking value. A/B/C standing choices are not meaningful unless learners must justify, construct, repair, or use evidence.

## Homework Route

Assign from the same Practice Book, but keep the decision in the Teacher Guide. State:

- exercise numbers/pages;
- 15–30 minute estimate;
- taught language prerequisite;
- whether the task is written or oral;
- teacher-verifiable oral completion route;
- next-lesson retrieval, checking, revision, or reuse.

Do not assign the exact classwork exercise again as homework. If an unfinished class product remains on its Practice Book page, continue there rather than duplicating it.

## Answers and Judgment

Provide:

- exact answers for closed tasks;
- acceptable variations for sentence building;
- target form plus meaning requirements for repair/transformation;
- evidence requirements for reading judgments;
- functional criteria for retell, report, speaking, or writing;
- common wrong answers and the shortest useful correction when likely.

Keep answers close to the lesson or in one clearly keyed compact section. Never leave the teacher to reverse-engineer the expected response from a generated worksheet.
