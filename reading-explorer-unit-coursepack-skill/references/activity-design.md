# Activity Design

## Purpose Before Novelty

Use games, movement, competition, collaboration, and creative work to rehearse a specific reading, vocabulary, grammar, listening, speaking, or retelling objective. Do not add an activity solely to make a page look varied.

Do not set a game quota. Select active practice when it serves the current language, reading, listening, retell, or output goal. Across a complete Unit, vary the cognitive action and activity family enough that learners do not repeat the same mechanic with new decoration.

## Activity Contract

Materialize every selected activity with:

- learning target and prerequisite;
- family and activity type;
- grouping and roles;
- materials/setup;
- time range;
- learner directions using `WHO + ACTION + TIME + PRODUCT + CHECK`;
- group product;
- individual evidence from every learner;
- answer/check move;
- access support and extension;
- noise, movement, space, and safety notes;
- low-preparation fallback.

Store these fields inside the relevant lesson record in the coursepack specification.

Do not treat confident volunteers or the fastest group as evidence for the class.

- Sentence Reconstruction Relay
- Human Sentence / Clause Line-Up

- Four Corners with Evidence and Justification
- Gallery Walk

### Competition and retrieval

- Board Race
- Vocabulary Relay
- Error Auction
- Grammar Detective
- Evidence Hunt
- Quiz Stations

### Information and collaboration

- Jigsaw Reading
- Information Gap
- Expert Group Exchange
- Card Sort and Justify
- Paragraph Puzzle

- Comic Strip Reconstruction
- Storyboard

- Dictogloss
- Listen and Draw/Order
- Shadowing Relay
- Find the Difference by Listening
- Pair Rehearsal Ladder
- Recorded One-Minute Retell

## Variety Rules

- Vary cognitive action as well as surface format: retrieve, classify, sequence, infer, justify, reconstruct, create, evaluate.
- Mix individual, pair, team, and whole-class participation.
- Give speaking tasks quiet planning and rehearsal before public output.
- Add a personal recording line, answer slip, mini-whiteboard response, exit item, or workbook entry to capture individual evidence.
- Provide a seated/low-noise fallback for rooms where running or movement is unsafe.
- Never require speed as the only success criterion; accuracy, language construction, repair, or evidence must affect the result.
- Keep team points teacher-adjudicated. Do not print learner/team self-scoring boxes in the Practice Book.
- Keep setup, grouping, rules, timing, and scoring in the Teacher Guide. Print only the individual preparation or record that remains useful after the activity.
