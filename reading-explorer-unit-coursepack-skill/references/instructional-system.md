# Instructional System

## Unit Role

Treat Reading Explorer as a second-language course. Topic knowledge creates meaning and communication need; vocabulary, chunks, sentence form, reading skill, listening, speaking, writing, retrieval, and transfer are the learning system.

## Stable Unit Architecture

Use this default capacity model for a Grade 6 Unit:

- Reading A — Deep Reading: 7–8 lessons;
- Reading B — Transfer Reading: 3–4 lessons;
- video/listening: about 2 lessons;
- integrated output: about 1–2 lessons;
- retrieval, feedback, and reteaching: about 1 lesson.

Aim for roughly sixteen 40-minute periods, but let source difficulty determine the final count. Do not delete a functional component merely to hit sixteen.

## Reading A — Deep Reading

Use:

`context/prediction -> meaning-first reading -> comprehension/evidence/Reading Skill -> language mining -> focus on form -> controlled/guided use -> mini-output -> delayed retrieval`

Keep the first two lessons meaning-first. Spread comprehension across both; do not force every gist, detail, inference, evidence, and critical-thinking item into Lesson 1.

After comprehension, divide sentence-by-sentence study by language density. A simple sentence may take one minute; a dense sentence may require meaning recovery, vocabulary, chunks, form, repair, and new use. Cover every sentence in the teacher ledger without giving every paragraph equal time.

End Reading A with cumulative language retrieval and an accountable retell/transfer lesson.

## Reading B — Transfer Reading

Use:

`independent comprehension -> reuse Reading Skill -> selective new language -> reuse Reading A language -> transfer check`

Increase learner independence. Do not repeat the complete Reading A close-study cycle. Select fewer new active language items and require reuse of Reading A chunks, patterns, evidence moves, or discourse functions.

## Video and Listening

- Set a first-view/listen purpose.
- Check gist before detail.
- Use a second listen for sequence, evidence, or key language.
- Provide transcript support after listening unless accessibility requires earlier support.
- Include oral reconstruction, shadowing, dictogloss, sequencing, or explanation only when it serves the Unit language.
- Provide a verified teacher read-aloud fallback when media may fail.

## Active Language Selection

Select a word, chunk, or pattern as active when it is:

- needed for retell, discussion, or writing;
- transferable beyond the article;
- connected to a useful collocation, word family, chunk, or frame;
- recyclable later in the Unit;
- appropriate and teachable within available time.

Prefer usable units such as `suggest that...`, `explain why...`, `be considered`, or `can no longer be seen` over isolated words.

At first encounter, provide:

1. source meaning;
2. age-appropriate English definition;
3. pronunciation/spelling/form only when needed;
4. chunk/collocation;
5. controlled accuracy check;
6. new-context guided use;
7. later retrieval.

Keep active/recognition decisions teacher-facing.

## Grammar, Sentence, and Discourse

Choose grammar that improves comprehension or useful production. Do not turn every feature in a sentence into a student objective.

For a productive feature:

`source meaning -> notice form/function -> controlled choice -> repair/transform -> guided sentence -> independent new context -> later retrieval`

Use sentence and paragraph functions as output scaffolds: introduce a phenomenon, add evidence, propose the simplest explanation, contrast new evidence, offer another possibility, and state an unknown point.

## Speaking and Retell

Use:

`input -> keywords/structure -> quiet preparation -> pair rehearsal -> teacher-verifiable/public output -> feedback -> retry`

Peer rehearsal prepares students but does not prove completion. Capture teacher recitation, random check, recorded evidence, or another teacher-verifiable route.

## Writing

Use:

`model -> notice -> plan -> draft -> feedback -> revise -> publish or transfer`

Do not assign open writing without a model, plan, language support, response space, and criteria.

## Homework

Give one assignment after each lesson when the course routine requires daily homework. Keep each assignment 15–30 minutes and tied to taught content.

Homework may retrieve, practice, complete, rehearse, revise, or transfer. It may not introduce a new article, word set, grammar form, or unfamiliar task type.

Lessons 1–2 may use meaning/evidence homework. Close-study lessons use delayed vocabulary/chunk/form retrieval, repair, and new context. Later lessons use retell, text building, mixed retrieval, and revision.

## Activity Quality

Do not require a game every lesson.

Use an activity only when it adds language work. Require a clear objective and at least two of:

- right/wrong judgment or scoring;
- evidence;
- construction or repair;
- strategic choice;
- time pressure;
- competition.

Every group task ends with individual written or oral evidence. Teacher-adjudicated scoring may motivate; learner/team self-scoring does not replace evidence.

## Differentiation

Keep the same central objective while changing access or depth:

- access: word bank, chunking, worked example, sentence frame, visual, rehearsal, extra processing time;
- extension: less support, more evidence, explanation, comparison, audience change, or transfer.

Do not define differentiation as weaker learners doing fewer items and stronger learners doing more.
