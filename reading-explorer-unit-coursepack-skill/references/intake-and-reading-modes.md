# Structured Intake and Reading Modes

## Fixed source package

Treat the teacher's recurring upload as a structured package. Match every file to the requested Unit/article code before reading its content.

| Package slot | Typical material | Primary use |
|---|---|---|
| `01_textbook` | Student Book article pages; optional Teacher Book/answer pages | Article truth, textbook questions, captions, reading skill |
| `02_worksheet` | F-level exercise PDF plus answer PDF | Selectable comprehension, vocabulary, form, and review tasks |
| `03_bloom` | Article-specific Bloom taxonomy guide; general Bloom prompt guide | Short-answer comprehension, analysis, evaluation, critical thinking |
| `04_vocabulary` | Vocabulary list plus vocabulary test | Candidate word set, meanings, word forms, controlled practice |
| `05_pre_post` | Article-specific pre-reading and post-reading sheets | Optional activation, quick checks, review, or cloze source |
| `06_grammar_cloze` | Article grammar-cloze document | Optional extensive-reading opener or later text reconstruction |
| `07_official_extra` | Extra comprehension, additional reading, graphic organizer, video sheet, crossword | Selective enrichment; never automatic inclusion |
| `08_teacher_notes` | Close-reading notes, self-study pages, personal notes | Teacher insight and possible language targets; verify against article |
| `09_layout_authority` | Latest teacher-edited Practice Book or other approved sample | Page hierarchy, density, typography, spacing, and response-area preference |

Ask the teacher to upload the full package when possible. Record each slot as `found`, `not_supplied`, `not_applicable`, or `wrong_unit`. Do not repeatedly ask about a slot already present.

## Structured analysis record

Create a source inventory before exercise design. For every file record:

- package slot;
- filename and SHA-256;
- Unit/article code;
- source role;
- usable sections/pages;
- answer-key availability;
- layout or extraction concerns;
- planned use: `select`, `adapt`, `verify`, `style_only`, or `exclude`;
- exclusion reason when unused.

Then create a compact exercise-source map:

| Proposed Practice Book block | Preferred source route |
|---|---|
| Quick comprehension | Textbook or worksheet multiple choice |
| Deeper short answers | Article-specific Bloom guide, checked against article |
| Find words | Vocabulary list/worksheet meaning prompts, verified in article |
| Find phrases | Article language selected around useful words and sentence frames |
| Words in sentences | Vocabulary test plus newly authored article-grounded sentences |
| Sentence work | High-value article sentences and teacher notes |
| Grammar cloze | Supplied cloze/pre-post sheet, used only if it serves the reading mode |
| Summary/rebuild | Worksheet, pre/post material, or a source-grounded authored summary |
| Final report | Article structure plus Bloom questions and practised sentence frames |

This intake structure is fixed. The lesson design is not.

## Three mandatory teaching decisions

Confirm:

1. `reading_mode`: `intensive` or `extensive`;
2. `lesson_count` and `lesson_minutes`;
3. `teacher_flow`: the teacher's intended sequence in plain language.

Use the teacher's flow as a design constraint, not as a form to be mechanically reproduced. Acknowledge the plan, identify any missing prerequisite, and design lessons around the article's actual demands.

## Intensive reading

Intensive reading normally includes more rereading and cumulative language work, but the sequence may vary.

Useful components include:

- textbook multiple choice for fast comprehension;
- Bloom short answers for deeper reading;
- article-based word and phrase search;
- vocabulary in complete sentence contexts;
- high-value sentence frames through correction, combination, transformation, translation, or new sentence writing;
- summary cloze, text rebuilding, retell, or a short final report.

Do not require every component or a fixed number of lessons. Let the teacher's flow, the article, and the available source questions determine the route.

## Extensive reading

The teacher's proposed route is valid:

`brief source-grounded grammar cloze or prediction -> reveal the article -> timed fast reading -> simple comprehension -> selective vocabulary/phrase follow-up -> quick close`

Apply these guardrails:

- Use familiar grammar only. Do not introduce a new form before reading.
- Keep the cloze brief—normally 5–10 minutes and 6–12 purposeful gaps.
- Prefer a selected excerpt or concise source-grounded version over blanking the whole article.
- Choose gaps that support prediction, cohesion, tense awareness, or common function words; do not turn every content word into a test.
- Reveal the original article immediately afterward and give a clear timed-reading purpose.
- Use a small number of gist/detail questions. Do not reproduce the intensive-reading close-study cycle.
- Teach only a selective vocabulary/phrase set needed for comprehension or future reuse.
- Keep total pre-reading language load lower than the reading itself.

The cloze fails as an extensive-reading opener when students spend most of the lesson debating grammar, cannot read for meaning afterward, or meet untaught grammar/vocabulary in the gaps.

## Flexible lesson design

Do not impose one stage sequence on every lesson. Vary the design according to:

- reading mode;
- article length and difficulty;
- source exercise quality;
- lesson count;
- teacher's preferred entry point;
- need for comprehension, language practice, discussion, or output;
- homework routine.

Keep only these universal constraints: a clear reading/language purpose, teachable timing, observable student work, prepared homework, and a next-lesson check.
