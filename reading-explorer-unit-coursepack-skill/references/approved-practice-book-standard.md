## What Approval Covers

Approval covers:

- merging Student Workbook and Homework Booklet into one `Practice Book`;
- treating the book as a general printed exercise book rather than a record of one teacher's classroom events;
- the exercise density, flat hierarchy, short task instructions, adjacent response spaces, restrained color, and compact page use;
- the progression from vocabulary/chunks through form, sentence work, text building, review, evidence, and short transfer;
- keeping assignment decisions and classroom activity logistics in the Teacher Guide;
- removing self-check panels, learner-facing teaching metalanguage, build metadata, and teacher-specific scoring/check language.

Approval does not authorize a routing-only Teacher Guide. The Teacher Guide must still contain the complete design for every lesson, including its introduction/lead-in.

## Authority Hierarchy

Preserve this hierarchy:

1. Unit title and `Practice Book` identity.
2. Large learning section, such as Vocabulary and Collocations, Words in Use, Sentences and Grammar, Text Building, or Unit Review.
3. Numbered `Exercise N · Title` bar.
4. One direct English instruction with a short Chinese operational gloss when useful.
5. Word bank, example, item set, organizer, or response lines immediately below.

Do not insert a goal box, lesson metadata, source route, class procedure, self-check, pair-check, score box, reflection prompt, or next-lesson note between these layers.

## Approved Exercise Grammar

The authority demonstrated these task families:

1. English definitions;
2. fixed collocations/chunks;
3. words in complete new contexts;
4. correct word forms in sentence context;
5. find and repair one target-language error, rewriting the whole sentence;
6. combine ideas with a specified connector or pattern;
7. transform sentence form without changing meaning;
8. rebuild text order and identify discourse function;
9. complete a summary cloze using article logic and chunks;
10. mixed language review;
11. judge textual support and repair unsupported claims;
12. write a short functional report with specified sentence purposes.

These are an approved repertoire, not a twelve-exercise quota. Select only the families that serve the Unit's taught language and reading goals. Preserve the staircase and exercise clarity, not identical item counts.

## Approved Student Boundary

Student pages may show:

- language content and normal exercise terminology;
- a brief Chinese gloss where the operation might otherwise be unclear;
- `Name / Class / Date`;
- examples, word banks, options, tables, and writing lines needed for completion;
- ordinary labels such as `Evidence`, `Simplest idea`, or `Another possibility` when they are part of the student product.

Student pages must not show:

- `Productive`, `Recognition`, `Core active`, `Language target`, or selection rationale;
- `Today I will learn`, `Now I can`, `Self-check`, or generic reflection panels;
- `Teacher will score`, `Pair check`, `Team score`, `Random check`, `Tomorrow`, or teacher-only completion routes;
- `Assign after`, `Next class use`, checkpoint, source ID, lesson ID, pilot, build, or production metadata;
- complete teacher explanations, finished sentence analyses, or answer-filled notes.

## Visual Tokens

Use `assets/templates/docx-style-tokens.json` as the starting point.

- US Letter portrait, 8.5 × 11 inches.
- Side margins approximately 0.62 inch; top/bottom approximately 0.55 inch.
- Compact but readable density; normal student text normally 10–11 pt.
- Dark green for major headings and exercise bars; gold for instruction emphasis; pale fills only for functional word banks or short cases.
- One running header and a small page number.
- Flat page grammar: exercises and answer space dominate, not cards or interface furniture.
- High contrast and meaningful in grayscale.
- No all-capital display labels.

## Regression Test

Reject a new Practice Book when:

- it looks like a lesson plan, activity sheet, slide, app dashboard, or AI-generated checklist rather than a printed exercise book;
- the learner cannot identify the action and answer location immediately;
- an exercise is merely a classroom event written on paper;
- vocabulary appears only in a final review instead of at first encounter;
- word/form work is trivial and never progresses to repair, sentence use, or new context;
- reading comprehension crowds out vocabulary, chunks, sentences, and grammar;
- the answer space does not fit the requested product;
- classwork and homework repeat the same surface task or answer route.
